# Problem 1.19

!!! info "Textbook Reference"
    - **Textbook**: *Classical Mechanics* (John R. Taylor)
    - **Chapter**: 1 — Newton's Laws of Motion
    - **Problem**: 1.19
    - **Difficulty**: $\star\star$
    - **Topic**: Vector Calculus, Product Rule for Scalar Triple Product

---

## Problem Statement

If $\mathbf{r}$, $\mathbf{v}$, and $\mathbf{a}$ denote the position, velocity, and acceleration of a particle, prove that:

$$
\frac{d}{dt} \big[ \mathbf{a} \cdot (\mathbf{v} \times \mathbf{r}) \big] = \dot{\mathbf{a}} \cdot (\mathbf{v} \times \mathbf{r})
$$

---

## Solution

### 1. Product Rule for the Scalar Triple Product

Applying the product rule for the dot product of the vector functions $\mathbf{a}(t)$ and $(\mathbf{v} \times \mathbf{r})(t)$:

$$
\frac{d}{dt} \big[ \mathbf{a} \cdot (\mathbf{v} \times \mathbf{r}) \big] = \dot{\mathbf{a}} \cdot (\mathbf{v} \times \mathbf{r}) + \mathbf{a} \cdot \frac{d}{dt}(\mathbf{v} \times \mathbf{r})
$$

### 2. Differentiation of the Cross Product

Applying the vector product rule to the cross product term:

$$
\frac{d}{dt}(\mathbf{v} \times \mathbf{r}) = \dot{\mathbf{v}} \times \mathbf{r} + \mathbf{v} \times \dot{\mathbf{r}}
$$

Using the kinematic definitions $\dot{\mathbf{r}} = \mathbf{v}$ and $\dot{\mathbf{v}} = \mathbf{a}$:

$$
\frac{d}{dt}(\mathbf{v} \times \mathbf{r}) = \mathbf{a} \times \mathbf{r} + \mathbf{v} \times \mathbf{v}
$$

Because the cross product of any vector with itself vanishes identically ($\mathbf{v} \times \mathbf{v} = \mathbf{0}$):

$$
\frac{d}{dt}(\mathbf{v} \times \mathbf{r}) = \mathbf{a} \times \mathbf{r}
$$

### 3. Orthogonality and Final Evaluation

Substituting $\frac{d}{dt}(\mathbf{v} \times \mathbf{r}) = \mathbf{a} \times \mathbf{r}$ back into the total derivative:

$$
\frac{d}{dt} \big[ \mathbf{a} \cdot (\mathbf{v} \times \mathbf{r}) \big] = \dot{\mathbf{a}} \cdot (\mathbf{v} \times \mathbf{r}) + \mathbf{a} \cdot (\mathbf{a} \times \mathbf{r})
$$

By definition of the vector cross product, $\mathbf{a} \times \mathbf{r}$ is perpendicular to $\mathbf{a}$. Consequently, its scalar product with $\mathbf{a}$ vanishes:

$$
\mathbf{a} \cdot (\mathbf{a} \times \mathbf{r}) = 0
$$

The second term drops out identically, leaving:

$$
\frac{d}{dt} \big[ \mathbf{a} \cdot (\mathbf{v} \times \mathbf{r}) \big] = \dot{\mathbf{a}} \cdot (\mathbf{v} \times \mathbf{r})
$$
