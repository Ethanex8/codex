# Problem 1.1

!!! info "Textbook Reference"
    - **Textbook**: *Classical Mechanics* (John R. Taylor)
    - **Chapter**: 1 — Newton's Laws of Motion
    - **Section**: 1.2 — Space and Time
    - **Problem**: 1.1
    - **Difficulty**: $\star$
    - **Topic**: Vector addition, scalar multiplication, dot product, cross product

---

## Problem Statement

Given the two three-dimensional vectors:
$$
\mathbf{b} = \hat{\mathbf{x}} + \hat{\mathbf{y}} = (1, 1, 0)
$$
$$
\mathbf{c} = \hat{\mathbf{x}} + \hat{\mathbf{z}} = (1, 0, 1)
$$

Find:

1. $\mathbf{b} + \mathbf{c}$
2. $5\mathbf{b} + 2\mathbf{c}$
3. $\mathbf{b} \cdot \mathbf{c}$
4. $\mathbf{b} \times \mathbf{c}$

---

## Solution

In Cartesian components, we represent the vectors as:
$$
\mathbf{b} = (b_x, b_y, b_z) = (1, 1, 0)
$$
$$
\mathbf{c} = (c_x, c_y, c_z) = (1, 0, 1)
$$

### Part 1: Vector Addition $\mathbf{b} + \mathbf{c}$

Adding component-wise:
$$
\mathbf{b} + \mathbf{c} = (b_x + c_x)\hat{\mathbf{x}} + (b_y + c_y)\hat{\mathbf{y}} + (b_z + c_z)\hat{\mathbf{z}}
$$
$$
\mathbf{b} + \mathbf{c} = (1 + 1)\hat{\mathbf{x}} + (1 + 0)\hat{\mathbf{y}} + (0 + 1)\hat{\mathbf{z}} = 2\hat{\mathbf{x}} + \hat{\mathbf{y}} + \hat{\mathbf{z}}
$$

Or in triplet notation:
$$
\mathbf{b} + \mathbf{c} = (2, 1, 1)
$$

---

### Part 2: Linear Combination $5\mathbf{b} + 2\mathbf{c}$

Scale each vector by its scalar coefficient and sum:
$$
5\mathbf{b} = 5(1, 1, 0) = (5, 5, 0)
$$
$$
2\mathbf{c} = 2(1, 0, 1) = (2, 0, 2)
$$

Summing the components:
$$
5\mathbf{b} + 2\mathbf{c} = (5 + 2)\hat{\mathbf{x}} + (5 + 0)\hat{\mathbf{y}} + (0 + 2)\hat{\mathbf{z}} = 7\hat{\mathbf{x}} + 5\hat{\mathbf{y}} + 2\hat{\mathbf{z}}
$$

Or in triplet notation:
$$
5\mathbf{b} + 2\mathbf{c} = (7, 5, 2)
$$

---

### Part 3: Scalar (Dot) Product $\mathbf{b} \cdot \mathbf{c}$

The scalar product in Cartesian coordinates is given by the sum of the products of corresponding components:
$$
\mathbf{b} \cdot \mathbf{c} = b_x c_x + b_y c_y + b_z c_z
$$
$$
\mathbf{b} \cdot \mathbf{c} = (1)(1) + (1)(0) + (0)(1) = 1 + 0 + 0 = 1
$$

---

### Part 4: Vector (Cross) Product $\mathbf{b} \times \mathbf{c}$

The cross product can be computed using the formal determinant:
$$
\mathbf{b} \times \mathbf{c} = \begin{vmatrix}
\hat{\mathbf{x}} & \hat{\mathbf{y}} & \hat{\mathbf{z}} \\
b_x & b_y & b_z \\
c_x & c_y & c_z
\end{vmatrix} = \begin{vmatrix}
\hat{\mathbf{x}} & \hat{\mathbf{y}} & \hat{\mathbf{z}} \\
1 & 1 & 0 \\
1 & 0 & 1
\end{vmatrix}
$$

Expanding across the top row:
$$
\mathbf{b} \times \mathbf{c} = \hat{\mathbf{x}} \left( (1)(1) - (0)(0) \right) - \hat{\mathbf{y}} \left( (1)(1) - (0)(1) \right) + \hat{\mathbf{z}} \left( (1)(0) - (1)(1) \right)
$$
$$
\mathbf{b} \times \mathbf{c} = 1\hat{\mathbf{x}} - 1\hat{\mathbf{y}} - 1\hat{\mathbf{z}} = \hat{\mathbf{x}} - \hat{\mathbf{y}} - \hat{\mathbf{z}}
$$

Or in triplet notation:
$$
\mathbf{b} \times \mathbf{c} = (1, -1, -1)
$$
