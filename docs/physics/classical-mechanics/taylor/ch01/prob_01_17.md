# Problem 1.17

!!! info "Textbook Reference"
    - **Textbook**: *Classical Mechanics* (John R. Taylor)
    - **Chapter**: 1 — Newton's Laws of Motion
    - **Problem**: 1.17
    - **Difficulty**: $\star\star$
    - **Topic**: Cross Product, Distributive Property, Product Rule for Vector Differentiation

---

## Problem Statement

**(a)** Prove that the vector product $\mathbf{r} \times \mathbf{s}$ as defined by (1.9) is distributive; that is, that

$$
\mathbf{r} \times (\mathbf{u} + \mathbf{v}) = (\mathbf{r} \times \mathbf{u}) + (\mathbf{r} \times \mathbf{v})
$$

**(b)** Prove the product rule

$$
\frac{d}{dt}(\mathbf{r} \times \mathbf{s}) = \mathbf{r} \times \frac{d\mathbf{s}}{dt} + \frac{d\mathbf{r}}{dt} \times \mathbf{s}
$$

Be careful with the order of the factors.

---

## Solution

Recall the Cartesian component definition (1.9) of the vector cross product for any vectors $\mathbf{a}$ and $\mathbf{b}$:

$$
\mathbf{a} \times \mathbf{b} = (a_y b_z - a_z b_y)\,\hat{\mathbf{x}} + (a_z b_x - a_x b_z)\,\hat{\mathbf{y}} + (a_x b_y - a_y b_x)\,\hat{\mathbf{z}}
$$

### Part (a): Distributivity of the Cross Product

Let $\mathbf{w} = \mathbf{u} + \mathbf{v}$, whose components are $w_x = u_x + v_x$, $w_y = u_y + v_y$, and $w_z = u_z + v_z$.

We evaluate the Cartesian components of $\mathbf{r} \times (\mathbf{u} + \mathbf{v}) = \mathbf{r} \times \mathbf{w}$:

1. **$x$-component**:

$$
[\mathbf{r} \times (\mathbf{u} + \mathbf{v})]_x = r_y w_z - r_z w_y = r_y (u_z + v_z) - r_z (u_y + v_y)
$$

   Using the distributive law of ordinary real numbers:

$$
[\mathbf{r} \times (\mathbf{u} + \mathbf{v})]_x = (r_y u_z - r_z u_y) + (r_y v_z - r_z v_y) = (\mathbf{r} \times \mathbf{u})_x + (\mathbf{r} \times \mathbf{v})_x
$$

2. **$y$-component**:

$$
[\mathbf{r} \times (\mathbf{u} + \mathbf{v})]_y = r_z w_x - r_x w_z = r_z (u_x + v_x) - r_x (u_z + v_z)
$$

$$
[\mathbf{r} \times (\mathbf{u} + \mathbf{v})]_y = (r_z u_x - r_x u_z) + (r_z v_x - r_x v_z) = (\mathbf{r} \times \mathbf{u})_y + (\mathbf{r} \times \mathbf{v})_y
$$

3. **$z$-component**:

$$
[\mathbf{r} \times (\mathbf{u} + \mathbf{v})]_z = r_x w_y - r_y w_x = r_x (u_y + v_y) - r_y (u_x + v_x)
$$

$$
[\mathbf{r} \times (\mathbf{u} + \mathbf{v})]_z = (r_x u_y - r_y u_x) + (r_x v_y - r_y v_x) = (\mathbf{r} \times \mathbf{u})_z + (\mathbf{r} \times \mathbf{v})_z
$$

Since the equality holds for all three components:

$$
\mathbf{r} \times (\mathbf{u} + \mathbf{v}) = (\mathbf{r} \times \mathbf{u}) + (\mathbf{r} \times \mathbf{v})
$$

---

### Part (b): Product Rule for Differentiation

For time-dependent vectors $\mathbf{r}(t)$ and $\mathbf{s}(t)$, we differentiate each component of $\mathbf{r} \times \mathbf{s}$ with respect to $t$:

1. **$x$-component**:

$$
\frac{d}{dt}[\mathbf{r} \times \mathbf{s}]_x = \frac{d}{dt}(r_y s_z - r_z s_y)
$$

   Applying the product rule of single-variable calculus to each scalar product:

$$
\frac{d}{dt}[\mathbf{r} \times \mathbf{s}]_x = \left( r_y \frac{ds_z}{dt} + \frac{dr_y}{dt} s_z \right) - \left( r_z \frac{ds_y}{dt} + \frac{dr_z}{dt} s_y \right)
$$

   Regrouping terms according to which vector is differentiated:

$$
\frac{d}{dt}[\mathbf{r} \times \mathbf{s}]_x = \left( r_y \frac{ds_z}{dt} - r_z \frac{ds_y}{dt} \right) + \left( \frac{dr_y}{dt} s_z - \frac{dr_z}{dt} s_y \right)
$$

   From definition (1.9), the first term is the $x$-component of $\mathbf{r} \times \frac{d\mathbf{s}}{dt}$ and the second term is the $x$-component of $\frac{d\mathbf{r}}{dt} \times \mathbf{s}$:

$$
\frac{d}{dt}[\mathbf{r} \times \mathbf{s}]_x = \left( \mathbf{r} \times \frac{d\mathbf{s}}{dt} \right)_x + \left( \frac{d\mathbf{r}}{dt} \times \mathbf{s} \right)_x
$$

2. **$y$-component**:

$$
\frac{d}{dt}[\mathbf{r} \times \mathbf{s}]_y = \frac{d}{dt}(r_z s_x - r_x s_z) = \left( r_z \frac{ds_x}{dt} + \frac{dr_z}{dt} s_x \right) - \left( r_x \frac{ds_z}{dt} + \frac{dr_x}{dt} s_z \right)
$$

$$
= \left( r_z \frac{ds_x}{dt} - r_x \frac{ds_z}{dt} \right) + \left( \frac{dr_z}{dt} s_x - \frac{dr_x}{dt} s_z \right) = \left( \mathbf{r} \times \frac{d\mathbf{s}}{dt} \right)_y + \left( \frac{d\mathbf{r}}{dt} \times \mathbf{s} \right)_y
$$

3. **$z$-component**:

$$
\frac{d}{dt}[\mathbf{r} \times \mathbf{s}]_z = \frac{d}{dt}(r_x s_y - r_y s_x) = \left( r_x \frac{ds_y}{dt} + \frac{dr_x}{dt} s_y \right) - \left( r_y \frac{ds_x}{dt} + \frac{dr_y}{dt} s_x \right)
$$

$$
= \left( r_x \frac{ds_y}{dt} - r_y \frac{ds_x}{dt} \right) + \left( \frac{dr_x}{dt} s_y - \frac{dr_y}{dt} s_x \right) = \left( \mathbf{r} \times \frac{d\mathbf{s}}{dt} \right)_z + \left( \frac{d\mathbf{r}}{dt} \times \mathbf{s} \right)_z
$$

Recombining the vector components gives:

$$
\frac{d}{dt}(\mathbf{r} \times \mathbf{s}) = \mathbf{r} \times \frac{d\mathbf{s}}{dt} + \frac{d\mathbf{r}}{dt} \times \mathbf{s}
$$

Because the cross product is anticommutative ($\mathbf{a} \times \mathbf{b} = -\mathbf{b} \times \mathbf{a}$), the relative order of the factors in each product is critical and must be strictly preserved.
