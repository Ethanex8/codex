# Problem 1.23

!!! info "Textbook Reference"
    - **Textbook**: *Classical Mechanics* (John R. Taylor)
    - **Chapter**: 1 — Newton's Laws of Motion
    - **Problem**: 1.23
    - **Difficulty**: $\star\star$
    - **Topic**: Vector Algebra, Vector Triple Product, BAC-CAB Rule

## Problem Statement

The unknown vector $\mathbf{v}$ satisfies $\mathbf{b} \cdot \mathbf{v} = \lambda$ and $\mathbf{b} \times \mathbf{v} = \mathbf{c}$, where $\lambda$, $\mathbf{b}$, and $\mathbf{c}$ are fixed and known. Find $\mathbf{v}$ in terms of $\lambda$, $\mathbf{b}$, and $\mathbf{c}$.

## Solution

We are given two equations for the unknown vector $\mathbf{v}$:

$$
\mathbf{b} \cdot \mathbf{v} = \lambda
$$

$$
\mathbf{b} \times \mathbf{v} = \mathbf{c}
$$

Taking the vector cross product of $\mathbf{b}$ with both sides of the second equation from the left:

$$
\mathbf{b} \times (\mathbf{b} \times \mathbf{v}) = \mathbf{b} \times \mathbf{c}
$$

Applying the vector triple product identity ($\mathbf{A} \times (\mathbf{B} \times \mathbf{C}) = \mathbf{B}(\mathbf{A} \cdot \mathbf{C}) - \mathbf{C}(\mathbf{A} \cdot \mathbf{B})$):

$$
\mathbf{b} (\mathbf{b} \cdot \mathbf{v}) - \mathbf{v} (\mathbf{b} \cdot \mathbf{b}) = \mathbf{b} \times \mathbf{c}
$$

Substituting $\mathbf{b} \cdot \mathbf{v} = \lambda$ and $\mathbf{b} \cdot \mathbf{b} = b^2$:

$$
\lambda \mathbf{b} - b^2 \mathbf{v} = \mathbf{b} \times \mathbf{c}
$$

Isolating the term containing $\mathbf{v}$:

$$
b^2 \mathbf{v} = \lambda \mathbf{b} - \mathbf{b} \times \mathbf{c} = \lambda \mathbf{b} + \mathbf{c} \times \mathbf{b}
$$

Assuming $\mathbf{b} \neq \mathbf{0}$ and dividing by $b^2$:

$$
\mathbf{v} = \frac{\lambda \mathbf{b} - \mathbf{b} \times \mathbf{c}}{b^2} = \frac{\lambda \mathbf{b} + \mathbf{c} \times \mathbf{b}}{b^2}
$$

This expression uniquely determines $\mathbf{v}$ as the sum of its component parallel to $\mathbf{b}$ and its component perpendicular to $\mathbf{b}$:

$$
\mathbf{v}_\parallel = \frac{\lambda}{b^2} \mathbf{b}, \quad \mathbf{v}_\perp = \frac{\mathbf{c} \times \mathbf{b}}{b^2}
$$
