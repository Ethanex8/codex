# Problem 1.4

!!! info "Textbook Reference"

    - **Topic**: Scalar product, angle between vectors

## Problem Statement

Find the angle between the vectors:

$$
\mathbf{b} = (1, 2, 4) \quad \text{and} \quad \mathbf{c} = (4, 2, 1)
$$

by evaluating their scalar product.

---

## Solution

The scalar product of two vectors can be written geometrically in terms of the angle $\theta$ between them:

$$
\mathbf{b} \cdot \mathbf{c} = |\mathbf{b}| |\mathbf{c}| \cos \theta
$$

and in terms of Cartesian components:

$$
\mathbf{b} \cdot \mathbf{c} = b_x c_x + b_y c_y + b_z c_z
$$

### 1. Component Scalar Product

Evaluating $\mathbf{b} \cdot \mathbf{c}$ directly from the components:

$$
\mathbf{b} \cdot \mathbf{c} = (1)(4) + (2)(2) + (4)(1) = 4 + 4 + 4 = 12
$$

### 2. Vector Magnitudes

The magnitudes of $\mathbf{b}$ and $\mathbf{c}$ are:

$$
|\mathbf{b}| = \sqrt{1^2 + 2^2 + 4^2} = \sqrt{1 + 4 + 16} = \sqrt{21}
$$

$$
|\mathbf{c}| = \sqrt{4^2 + 2^2 + 1^2} = \sqrt{16 + 4 + 1} = \sqrt{21}
$$

### 3. Angle Between the Vectors

Equating the two forms for the scalar product gives:

$$
\sqrt{21} \cdot \sqrt{21} \cos \theta = 12
$$

$$
21 \cos \theta = 12 \implies \cos \theta = \frac{12}{21} = \frac{4}{7}
$$

Taking the inverse cosine:

$$
\theta = \arccos\left(\frac{4}{7}\right) \approx 0.963 \text{ rad} \approx 55.2^\circ
$$
